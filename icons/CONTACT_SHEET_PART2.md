# Contact Sheet Part 2: icon candidates for chapters 4 to 7 of *Derech Tevunos*

Companion to `CH1-3_ICONS_EXPLORATION.md` (Part 1) and to
`SUGYA_WATERFALL_VISUALIZATION_V1.md`. Part 1 covered the parties in debate and
the anatomy of one statement. This sheet covers chapters 4 to 7: how two
statements relate, what one statement implies, when a statement is not meant
literally, and how a new statement is derived from an old one. The SVGs live in
`icons-ch4-7/`, generated by `gen_icons_part2.py` from the shared `iconlib.py`.

Page numbers follow the bilingual edition: even pages English, odd pages Hebrew.

## 1. These icons live on edges, not rows

Part 1's badges describe a single row. Nearly everything in chapters 4 to 7 is
about *two* statements, so these badges belong on the connector between two rows
of the waterfall, or on the row that a connector delivers. Each family annotates
a chapter 9 move that already exists in the model:

| Family | Annotates | Says |
|---|---|---|
| ch4 relations (`equivalent` … `incongruent`) | a `רמיא` or `סתירה` edge | how the two statements actually relate: head-on, general against particular, only sharing a part |
| ch4 tests (`differs-in-time` … `homonym`) | the `ישוב` that dissolves a `רמיא` | which of Ramchal's four tests did the work (Eng p54–56) |
| ch4 `no-middle` / `has-middle` | a `disjunction` row | whether eliminating one branch can prove the other (Eng p60, used at p108) |
| ch5 inferences | a `statement/inference` (`דיוק`) row | whether the inference is forced or merely suggested by the wording |
| ch6 `figurative` | a `statement` row | that its truth turns on the allusion, not the words (Eng p76) |
| ch7 syllogisms | a `proof/demonstration` edge | which kind of deduction the proof is |
| ch7 fallacies | a `difficulty/objection` edge against such a proof | why the analogy or a fortiori fails (Eng p100–104) |

This is the payoff. The waterfall today says *that* a proof was brought and
*that* an objection landed. These badges say *what kind* of proof and *which
way* it broke, which is what a learner actually asks.

## 2. Colour: Ramchal's own partition

Chapter 2 splits the mind's work into three processes (Eng p18–20):
understanding statements (chapters 3–6), deriving new ones (chapter 7), and
accepting or rejecting them (chapter 8). Colour follows that split:

- violet, chapters 3–6, the Part 1 hue kept for chapters 4–6;
- teal, chapter 7;
- chapter 8 will take a third hue when it is drawn;
- green and red stay reserved for verdicts, as in Part 1.

## 3. Same primitive, one step further

Every Part 2 glyph is a Part 1 glyph pushed one step, so a reader who learned
Part 1 pays nothing for Part 2:

| Part 1 | Part 2 | The step |
|---|---|---|
| `comparative`: ■ = □ (*just as… so too*) | `analogism`: ■ → □ | the statement form becomes the inference that uses it |
| `hypothetical`: two standing dominoes, arrow | `hypothetical-syllogism`: first domino filled | the link is now used, one side is established |
| `consequent`: fallen dominoes | `hypothetical-syllogism-tollens`: both crossed, arrow back | that did not fall, so this never did |
| `disjunction`: open fork | `disjunctive-syllogism`: one branch struck, other filled | not that one, so this one |
| `partial`: some dots filled | `absolute-opposite`: some dots, some dashes | some do, so some do not |
| filled = known, outline = unknown | kept throughout chapter 7 | filled is what you have, outline is what you derive |
| (new) negative rendering | `contrapositive`, `obverse` | drawn in negative means the signs are flipped |

## 4. The candidate set

### Chapter 4: how two statements relate (Eng p48–64)

| Key | English | Hebrew (Heb p) | Icon | Reads as |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `equivalent` | equivalent | דומים (47) | two stacked copies | the same thing, said twice |
| `variant` | variant | מתחלפים (51) | two circles, shared lens | share a part, differ in a part |
| `diametrically-opposed` | diametrically opposed | הפכיים ממש (55) | two equal arrows head-on | yes against no, same scope |
| `contradictory` | contradictory | מתנגדים (55) | a big arrow against a small one | a general claim against a particular one |
| `converse` | complete converse | חלוף כולל (61, 71) | swap arrows | subject and predicate trade places |
| `converse-limited` | limited converse | חלוף קצתי (61, 71) | swap, dashed return | swapped, but it only comes back for some |
| `contrapositive` | contrapositive converse | חלוף הפכי כולל (61, 71) | swap arrows in negative | swapped and both flipped to no |
| `obverse` | obverse | מתהפכים (63) | negative-image pair | both flipped to their opposites, same picture |
| `incongruent` | incongruent | נבדלים (63) | a circle and a square, apart | nothing in common |

The tests at Eng p54–56 that show two statements only *look* opposed:

| Key | Ramchal's test | Icon |
|---|---|---|
| `differs-in-time` | not the same time | clock |
| `differs-in-place` | not the same place (public domain vs courtyard, Shabbos 57a) | map pin |
| `differs-in-context` | not the same respect, בחינה (sewn in vs not, Shabbos 57a) | dashed frame around a dot |
| `homonym` | a term used in two senses ("blow", Eruvin 102b vs Rosh Hashanah 29b) | one square, two faces |

And the note at Eng p60 on contradictory terms: `no-middle` (clean / unclean:
two cells) and `has-middle` (optional / praiseworthy / obligatory: three cells).

### Chapter 5: what a statement implies (Eng p66–74)

| Key | English | Hebrew (Heb p) | Icon | Reads as |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `inference-necessary` | logically necessary inference | מוכרח (71) | two interlocked links | cannot accept the one and reject the other |
| `inference-loose` | inference not logically necessary | בלתי מוכרח (69) | links pulled apart, one dashed | suggested by the wording, but separable |
| `absolute-opposite` | absolute opposite (from a partial statement) | הפך (73) | two dots, two dashes | some do, so some do not |

The table of necessary inferences at Eng p72–74 needs no further glyphs: each
entry is one of the converse icons above, chosen by the quantity and sign of the
source statement. That table is a lookup a widget should compute, not a set of
badges a reader memorises.

### Chapter 6: not meant literally (Eng p76)

| Key | Icon | Reads as |
|---|---|---|
| `figurative` | speech bubble with a wave | not literal: judge the allusion ("a lion has come from Babylon") |

Literal is the default and gets no badge. The rest of chapter 6, the ultimate
intention of each statement type, is already encoded in Part 1 as the number of
separable parts each icon has.

### Chapter 7: deriving a conclusion (Eng p92–110)

| Key | English | Hebrew (Heb p) | Marker | Icon | Reads as |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `syllogism` | syllogism, premise → conclusion | הקש, הקדמה, תולדה (93) | | an arithmetic sum | premises added up give a result |
| `classical-syllogism` | classical syllogism | הקש מופתי (93) | | tree, top node filled, one leaf filled | true of the whole kind, so true of this member |
| `analogism` | analogism | בנין אב / מה מצינו (95) | מה מצינו | ■ → □ | found in this one, so in that similar one |
| `a-fortiori` | a fortiori | קל וחומר / כל שכן (97) | כל שכן | small dot → big circle | if the light case, then surely the heavy one |
| `hypothetical-syllogism` | hypothetical syllogism | הקש תלוי (105) | | filled domino → outlined domino | this is now known, so that follows |
| `hypothetical-syllogism-tollens` | the same, on the denial | הקש תלוי (105) | | both dominoes crossed, arrow back | that did not follow, so this was never so |
| `disjunctive-syllogism` | disjunctive syllogism | הקש מחלק (109) | | fork, one branch struck | not that one, so it must be this one |

Why an analogism or a fortiori fails (Eng p100–104, Heb p99):

| Key | Ramchal's condition | Icon |
|---|---|---|
| `fallacy-not-similar` | the two subjects are not really similar (Kerisos 26a, "מה לנשיא שכן…") | ■, broken arrow, ○ |
| `fallacy-not-greater` | what was taken as greater or lesser is not (Horayos 10a) | two equal circles, arrows both ways |
| `fallacy-counterexample` | another similar subject lacks the predicate (Toras Cohanim, the Cohen Gadol) | ■ → □, and a third □ crossed |

## 5. Attested fixtures

Ramchal labels every one of these passages himself, so each can double as a
test in the sense of V1 Part VII:

- Shabbos 57a, frontlets and hair net: only apparently opposed, by context and by place (Eng p54).
- Eruvin 102b against Rosh Hashanah 29b, "blow": a homonym (Eng p56).
- Shabbos 124a and Shabbos 28b: diametrically opposed, general and particular respectively (Eng p56–58).
- Shabbos 76b, husks of lentils: contradictory, general against particular (Eng p58).
- Yebamos 66a, eating and conferring terumah: limited converse, contrapositive, obverse (Eng p62–64).
- Berachos 53a, half Jewish and half not: a loose inference, rejected (Eng p70–72).
- Chagigah 15b, clean wool dyes completely: a forced inference, the contrapositive (Eng p72).
- Toras Cohanim, doubtful guilt offering: the same law derived first by analogism, then a fortiori (Eng p98).
- Kerisos 26a, Horayos 10a, Toras Cohanim: the three fallacies, one each (Eng p100–104).
- Pesachim 19a, Rabbi Yosi and Rabbi Akiva: hypothetical syllogism on the denial (Eng p106).
- Pesachim 5b and Bava Kamma 104a: disjunctive syllogism (Eng p108–110).

## 6. Gaps recorded, not resolved

- Chapter 4 opens by naming its third type "contradictory" (Eng p48), then
  heads the section "Opposite Statements" and uses "contradictory" for one of
  its two sub-types (p56). The Hebrew is consistent: הפכיים for the family,
  הפכיים ממש and מתנגדים for the sub-types. Unlike Part 1, key on the Hebrew
  here.
- "Contrapositive converse" in chapter 4 and "contrapositive" in chapter 5 are
  the same relation (Heb p61 and p71). Chapter 5 adds a "limited contrapositive"
  (חלוף קצתי הפכי, Heb p73) for partial statements; it has no glyph of its own
  and would render as `contrapositive` with a "some" chip.
- Ramchal's "classical syllogism" is not the three-term Aristotelian figure. It
  is inheritance down the subject or the predicate (Eng p92–94), and the tree
  icon follows Ramchal. Do not "correct" it toward two premises and a middle term.
- The tests for real opposition at Eng p54 read "time, place and context" and
  "primary meanings". The Hebrew (Heb p53) is sharper: same time, same place,
  same respect (בחינה), and the plain sense "without homonymy or metaphor"
  (בלי שום שתוף והשאלה). `homonym` is the שתוף; the השאלה is already
  `figurative`. Note that the frontlets example is phrased "בזמן ש", the idiom
  for "in the case where", so it is a בחינה example despite the word for time.

## 7. Where I am least sure

- `equivalent` as two stacked copies could read as "redundant" rather than
  "said the same thing", which in the Talmud is a positive identification
  (Kesubos 36b, Eng p48). If it misreads, two identical filled dots with an
  equals sign is the fallback, at the cost of resembling `comparative`.
- `hypothetical-syllogism-tollens` is the busiest glyph in either part. A text
  chip on the plain `hypothetical-syllogism` badge may serve better.
- The three fallacy icons carry three elements each; `fallacy-counterexample`
  is at the legibility limit at 20px.
- `differs-in-context` is the vaguest of the four tests. Ramchal's word is
  בחינה, "respect" or "aspect", and a dashed frame is the best everyday picture
  I have for "the same thing, considered differently". Open to a better one.
- Whether chapter 4 badges sit on the `רמיא` edge or on the resolving `ישוב`
  is a layout question, not a taxonomy one. Both are defensible; the sheet
  assumes the edge.
