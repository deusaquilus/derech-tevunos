# Icon candidates for chapters 1 to 3 of *Derech Tevunos*

Companion to `SUGYA_WATERFALL_VISUALIZATION_V1.md`. That document derived seven
pictographs from chapter 9. This one explores the constructs of chapters 1 to 3
(the parties in debate, and the anatomy of a single statement) and proposes a
candidate icon for each. Nothing here is built into `viz/`; the SVGs in
`icons-ch1-3/` are proposals to react to, generated by `gen_icons.py` in the
same 24×24 format as the existing seven.

Page numbers follow the bilingual edition: even pages English, odd pages Hebrew.

## 1. These are a different family, not seven more siblings

Chapter 9 names what a sentence *does* to an earlier sentence (proves, objects,
settles). Chapter 3 names what a sentence *is made of*: a subject, a predicate,
and "the way it is said" (Eng p42). The V1 spec (Part I.2) refused to merge the
two levels, and that refusal still stands. So these icons are badges that sit
*on* a statement, never rows of their own in the waterfall. Three rules follow:

1. **One hue for the whole family.** Colour in the waterfall means "which move".
   The anatomy family is all violet so that in a mixed view the eye reads moves
   by colour and structure by shape. Green and red stay reserved for verdicts:
   a predicate "said as certain" (Eng p26) must not look "accepted" (ch8), and
   "said as impossible" must not look "rejected".
2. **Everyday connectives, not logic vocabulary.** The self-labelling finding
   from V1 (Part I.4) holds here too. Ramchal defines nearly every type by a
   stock word: *all*, *except*, *alone*, *provided that*, *if… then*, *either…
   or*, *not… but rather*, *even though*, *just as… so too*, *therefore*. A
   reader who has never heard "predicate" knows every one of those words, so
   each icon is the everyday picture of its word, and the gloss beside it is
   the word itself.
3. **One separable part per truth condition.** Chapter 6 says, per type, what
   must hold for the statement to be true: an exception statement has *two*
   intentions (the rule and the exception, Eng p80), a conditional has two
   (the predication and the condition, p82), a hypothetical has *one* (only the
   dependence, p82), a consequent has *three* (antecedent, consequent, and the
   link, p88). The icon for each type should have exactly that many visually
   separable pieces, because the payoff of labelling a statement's type is
   knowing which piece a later difficulty can land on. A difficulty against the
   *condition* of a conditional statement leaves the main predication standing
   (p82); the icon should be able to show that by lighting the switch and not
   the ring.

## 2. The candidate set

Marker words are quoted from the Hebrew column; type names and definitions from
the English column. "Parts" is the count of intentions chapter 6 assigns.

| `comparative` | comparative | מדמה (39) | כשם ש… כך | filled square = outlined square | just as this (known), so too that (unknown) | 1 (88) |
| `consequent` | consequent | נמשך (39) | לפיכך | two fallen dominoes | this happened, so that happened | 3 (88) |

Two pairs are deliberate:

- **Hypothetical vs consequent** is the distinction Ramchal draws at Eng p88:
  the hypothetical claims only the dependence, the consequent claims that both
  clauses are real and that one followed from the other. Standing dominoes with
  the arrow emphasised versus fallen dominoes is that exact distinction, and a
  child can read it.
- **Comparative** encodes his rule at Eng p40 that "a known subject is always
  taken as the basis of comparison": the known side is filled, the unknown side
  is an outline.

### Chapter 1: who is speaking (Eng p10)

These are speaker badges, not structure, so they use the statement icon's
slate rather than the family violet. The waterfall's `speaker` field already
exists; these would let it render without a name.

| Key | Reads as | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `party-group` | several people debating | "many rabbis debate one topic", p10 |
| `party-individual` | one person arguing both sides | "The same one who asks has given the answer", p10 |
| `party-talmud` | the Talmud itself asking and answering | "the Talmud itself questions and answers", p10 |

### Not iconised, on purpose

- **Style** (extensive, concise, literal, rhetorical, metaphorical; Eng p42).
  Ramchal's instruction is to *ignore* style and abstract the intended
  statement. The right artefact is not an icon but a two-column widget: raw
  words on one side, abstracted subject / predicate / type on the other.
- **The three processes of the mind** (Eng p18–20) map to chapter groups, not to
  sentences. They are tab labels for a future visualization, not badges.

## 3. An attested fixture already exists

Eng p44–46 is Ramchal labelling a live passage (Pesachim 7b, the blessing over
circumcision) sentence by sentence with chapter 3 types: an *exclusive*
statement, a *consequent* statement, a *preclusive* statement, a *simple*
statement. That is exactly the attestation V1 Part I.5 requires before a fixture
can double as a test. It should be the first fixture for any chapter 3 badge
work, and the abstraction widget above is literally what those pages do by hand.

## 4. Gaps recorded, not resolved

- The Hebrew word **מגבל** is used for two different types: the second type is
  described as "מיחד ומגבל" (Heb p25) and the fifth is named "מאמר מגבל"
  (Heb p29). The translator's term index (p260) lists מוגבל only at 25. Use
  the English names as enum keys; never key on the Hebrew here.
- The **preclusive** statement is given no Hebrew name (Heb p37).
- **Discrepancy** is "מכחיש" in the text (Heb p37) and "מכיש" in the index
  (p260); same word, defective spelling.
- Chapter 3 counts **eleven** predicate types; the chapter outline (p249–250)
  shows the same eleven. Disjunction is a sub-type of the seventh, not a
  twelfth.

## 5. Where I am least sure

- **Conditional** was first drawn as an asterisk ("conditions apply") and
  rejected. The replacement borrows the circuit-diagram switch: a ring (the
  circuit, the predication) broken at the top by an open blade (the condition).
  The circuit only runs once the switch closes. The two parts map exactly onto
  the two intentions at Eng p82, and the ring keeps it in the circle-shaped
  group with *only* and *except*. A power-button variant
  (`conditional-alt-power.svg`) is kept as a fallback: it is the most legible
  shape at 20px but reads as on/off rather than as a condition.
- **Umbrella** for "even though" is the most metaphorical choice left. A reader
  could take it for "protection". Fallback: two opposed arrows with a bridge
  drawn over them.
- **Signal bars** for the qualified statement treat certain / possible /
  doubtful / impossible as one scale. Ramchal calls it "the entire range from
  certainty to possibility and from doubt to impossibility" (Eng p28), which
  licenses the scale, but *possible* is modal and *doubtful* is epistemic. In a
  widget this glyph wants to be a slider with four detents rather than four
  separate badges.
- The staircase pair is leaf-level. V1 Part IV.1 ruled that icons live at
  element level, so these two should probably be text chips ("not only… but
  even") on the single `compound` badge rather than glyphs of their own.
- The 2×2 grids were chosen over 3×3 for legibility at 20px. At 3×3 the
  "some" and "one" states blur together.
