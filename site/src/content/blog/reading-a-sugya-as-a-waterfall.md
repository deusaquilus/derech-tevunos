<p class="callout-label">In short</p>

Chapter 9 of *Derech Tevunos* gives every sentence of a sugya the move it
makes. Drawing those moves as a staircase reads well for a short passage and
breaks on an opening sugya, where a difficulty raised in the fourth row is
answered in the thirty-first. Two devices carry the long case: a **rail** for a
relation that reaches back many rows, and a **fold** that puts a settled
stretch behind a band.

</div>

Ramchal's subject in *Derech Tevunos* is not what the Talmud concludes. It is
how a sugya *moves* — what each sentence does to the one before it, and what
remains standing at the end. Chapter 9 gives that a closed vocabulary: seven
elements and nineteen leaves, each a thing a sentence can be doing.

That vocabulary is what makes a drawing possible. If every sentence carries a
label, and every label says what it acts upon, then the passage has a shape
independent of its content, and the shape can be put on a page.

## The staircase, and where it stops working

The obvious drawing is a staircase. One row per sentence, indented by how deep
into the argument it sits: a difficulty steps in from the statement it troubles,
a resolution steps back out. Ramchal's own worked examples are short enough that
this is the whole picture.

An opening sugya is not short. In *Bava Metzia* 21b-22b the argument runs
fifty-seven sentences: Abaye's claim in the first row comes under attack ten
separate times, and the support that finally settles it does not arrive until
the fifty-first. On a staircase that connector has nowhere sensible to go: draw
it straight and it crosses forty-nine unrelated rows; leave it out and the
reader cannot see that the claim was ever supported.

## What the rail does

A **rail** is the connector for exactly that case — one relation that reaches
far enough back that a local elbow would lie about the distance. It runs up the
gutter beside the rows rather than through them, in a colour no verdict uses, so
following it is never confused with the red of a refutation or the amber of a
doubt.

## What the fold does

A **fold** is different. It is not a connector but a container: a band that
takes a settled stretch of argument and puts it behind a summary the reader can
open.

The important property is that a fold is **not** a collapse. The band keeps the
verdict of everything inside it, so a reader who never opens it still knows that
the stretch settled and which way it went. That is what makes folding safe — the
reader loses the steps, not the result.

Both devices exist because the alternative is a page nobody can read. Neither
of them adds information; they only decide what the reader has to look at first.
